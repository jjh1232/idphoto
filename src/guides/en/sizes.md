"Passport size" means different things in different countries. Here are the common ones and the rules attached to them.

## Sizes at a glance

| Use | Size | Head size rule |
|---|---|---|
| US passport & visa | 2 × 2 in (5.08 × 5.08 cm) | 1–1⅜ in chin to top of head |
| UK, Schengen, Korea, Japan passports | 35 × 45 mm (3.5 × 4.5 cm) | Korea & Japan: 32–36 mm |
| Chinese visa | 33 × 48 mm | set by the embassy |
| Résumé photo (Korea, Japan) | 30 × 40 mm | none — custom, not a rule |

Always check the official page for the country you are applying to; the table covers the common cases, not every one.

## The US 2×2 photo in detail

- **Size:** 2 × 2 inches — 51 × 51mm on most charts, exactly 50.8mm.
- **Head:** 1 to 1⅜ inches (25–35mm) from the bottom of the chin to the top of the head.
- **Eyes:** 1⅛ to 1⅜ inches (28–35mm) from the bottom of the photo.
- **Digital:** square, 600×600 to 1200×1200 pixels. See [size in pixels](pixels).
- Plain white or off-white background, taken within the last six months, no glasses.

The [ID photo cropper](../en) draws the head and eye bands for this size so you can line them up.

## 35×45mm passports

Korea's rules: 3–5mm of space above the head, 32–36mm from the top of the head to the chin, white background.
Japan's are similar, with 2–6mm above the head. Other 35×45 countries set their own head-size ranges — the frame is the same, the guide lines are not, so check before you print.

## What gets a photo rejected

Rules differ in detail, but these are rejected almost everywhere:

- Photos edited with filters or software, including AI editing — the US rejects these automatically since January 2026.
- Backgrounds that were removed or replaced digitally.
- Shadows on the background, glare on glasses, hair covering the eyes.

**Cropping and positioning are not edits.** Cutting an existing photo down to size is fine.
Brightness and contrast changes count as edits, which is why the cropper locks them for passport and visa sizes.
